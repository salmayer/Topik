    How we practice in the chat

    1. Send your voice memo. Open our Korean chat and say it again using the keyboard mic, or type it. Say it the way you did, mistakes included.
    2. Get corrections. I'll mark each mistake, explain why in one line, and give you a more natural version.
    3. Say the fixed version out loud twice. This is where the correction sticks.
    4. Translation drill. I give you 5 English sentences using only grammar you've already studied, mixed with that day's TTMIK, KGIU and HTSK points. Say each answer out loud first, then send it.
    5. Mini conversation. I ask you 3 questions in Korean. Answer in full sentences and use at least one connector: ~는데, ~아/어서, ~지만, ~(으)니까.
    6. Keep a mistake list. Write the corrections you get in the back of your notebook. Repeat mistakes become the next day's drill.

    To start, send: "오늘 연습하자" with your memo.

    • Keep a bad-day minimum

      2 TTMIK lessons and your vocab notebook still count as a day. Your streak survives, and missing one day doesn't turn into missing a week.

    • Work in Pomodoro blocks

      Use the Pomodoro timer above, from 25 minutes up to 1 hour. Put your phone in another room.

    • Set up tomorrow tonight

      Leave the book open on the right page and write the first task on a sticky note. Starting is the hard part, so make it take no effort.

    • Only look at Today

      Keep the full roadmap closed while you study. A long list makes it harder to start.

    • Study with someone

      Play a "study with me" video, or call a friend who's also working.

    • Switch things up when you're bored

      Change the order of your tasks, or swap in Iyagi or a drama clip. It still counts as long as it's Korean.

    • Use your runs

      Listen to TTMIK on easy and long runs. Skip it on interval days.

    • Sleep is part of the plan

      Memory locks in while you sleep. A late night of cramming costs you the next day.
